# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the code, not
about the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- Take a contribution as an offer of someone's unpaid time, and say so.
- Give criticism about the work, with a reason and, where you can, a fix.
- Take criticism the same way. A rejected pull request is not a rejected person.
- Assume the least dramatic explanation. Most of what reads as rudeness online
  is someone writing quickly in their third language.

## What is not

- Insults, harassment, or bringing someone's identity into a technical argument.
- Publishing anyone's personal information, including things they leaked into a
  fixture by accident. Tell them privately instead.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, brigading, or demanding
  free work from a maintainer.

## One thing specific to this project

This tool measures gambling losses, which means people using it are sometimes
losing money, and some of them are in real trouble. Do not use anyone's figures
to mock them. Do not turn a bug report into a lecture about their habits.

If you are the one in trouble, [BeGambleAware](https://www.begambleaware.org/)
and [Gamblers Anonymous](https://www.gamblersanonymous.org/) are free and
confidential. A spreadsheet of your losses is not a solution to them.

## Reporting

Email **info.m04studio@gmail.com**. It reaches the maintainer and nobody else.

Reports are read and acted on. Depending on what happened, that means a private
word, a deleted comment, or a block. The maintainer has the last say and will
not always explain the reasoning publicly, because doing so usually means
publishing the details of whoever reported it.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, and anywhere someone is representing this
project.
